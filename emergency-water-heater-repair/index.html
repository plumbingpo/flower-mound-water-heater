How to Tell Something Is Wrong

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Every tap running cold and the heater showing no signs of life
  • Standing water around the water heater that returns after you wipe it up
  • The smell of gas near the unit, a situation that calls for evacuating and phoning the utility or 911
  • The relief valve on the side of the tank dripping, hissing, or discharging
  • Scorch marks, soot, or flames rolling out near the burner access on a gas unit
  • Water at the tap that is suddenly scalding hot
  • An electric water heater that keeps tripping its circuit breaker
  • Loud popping, banging, or rumbling from the tank that has gotten worse quickly

Why Hire a Professional for Emergency Water Heater Repair?

Emergency calls bring together electricity, gas, heat, pressure, and water, which is exactly the mix that makes do-it-yourself work risky. An electric heater sits on a 240-volt circuit that should never be handled while standing in water. A gas heater has fuel piping, a burner, and a vent that can leak exhaust into the room if something is out of place. The tank itself holds scalding water under pressure, and a discharging relief valve is signaling an unsafe condition that must be solved, not silenced. Capping that valve, relighting next to a gas odor, or forcing a rusted connection are all common and dangerous mistakes.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and the right parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution rather than guessing, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Just as important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a modest repair bill versus the cost of a whole new unit. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Find and label the cold-water shutoff at the heater and the main house valve now, before you ever need them
  • Add a battery leak alarm or an automatic shutoff valve that closes the supply when it senses water
  • Check the temperature and pressure relief valve each year so you know it will work when it matters
  • Set the temperature around 120F, which protects against burns and slows tank wear
  • A drain pan with a proper drain line under the tank can turn a flood into a manageable puddle
  • Keep boxes, paint, gasoline, and cleaning products well away from a gas water heater
  • Annual flushing keeps sediment from causing the rumbling and hot spots that lead to leaks
