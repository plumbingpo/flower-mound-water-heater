Why do so many people switch to tankless? Mostly three things: hot water that keeps coming, lower standby energy use, and space. A tankless heater stays idle until you open a tap, then fires a high-output burner or electric element and heats the water in passing. With no stored water to keep warm, it wastes less energy between uses, and with regular descaling it can last two decades. It is not magic, though. Performance depends entirely on sizing and installation, which is where most of our work in Flower Mound, TX goes.

Sizing is where tankless installs succeed or fail. We work from two numbers, flow rate and temperature rise: add up the fixtures you realistically run at the same time in gallons per minute, then factor in how cold the incoming water gets in winter, since a unit that delivers five gallons per minute at a mild rise may deliver far less when the groundwater is cold. Then comes the infrastructure. A whole-home gas unit can draw 150,000 to 199,000 BTU, which often means a larger gas line or confirming the meter can keep up. Venting must match the unit, and electric tankless models can need a large share of your electrical service, sometimes enough to require a panel upgrade.

The small parts of the install matter for decades. We install service valves on every unit so descaling is quick, pipe the relief valve to a safe discharge point, build the gas connection with a sediment trap, and run venting exactly to the manufacturer's specifications. Hard water gets addressed up front with honest advice on scale protection, because scale is what clogs heat exchangers. Recirculation is available too if a far bathroom takes too long to get hot. Permits are pulled where required, and you get a walkthrough of settings, error codes, and upkeep. For tankless installation in Flower Mound, call (972) 975-6347.

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Why not install a tankless unit yourself? Because the risks and the details stack up quickly. Gas piping must be sized for a burner that may draw nearly 199,000 BTU, and leaks or undersized lines are both dangerous and costly. Venting has to match the unit exactly, with the right material, slope, and termination clearances, or combustion gases can end up where they do not belong. Electric units can overwhelm an older electrical panel, and warranty terms often depend on a qualified installation. Permits and inspections are common requirements as well.

Done professionally, a tankless system performs well and stays safe. We start with a real demand assessment, check the gas line, meter, and panel, and design venting that meets the manufacturer's requirements. The install includes service valves, a sediment trap, a correctly routed relief valve line, and a neutralized condensate drain where needed, then a test with several fixtures running. Just as important, we tell you plainly if a tank, hybrid, or smaller point-of-use unit would serve you better. The right fit matters more to us than the type of unit.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Protect outdoor or garage units from freezing, and keep power on during cold snaps so freeze protection works
  • Hard water shortens tankless life, so ask about scale protection
  • If the unit shows a recurring code, note it and have it checked
  • Set the temperature around 120F for safety and efficiency
  • Keep the air intake and vent termination clear of snow, leaves, and debris
  • Flush the unit with a descaling solution annually to keep scale off the heat exchanger
  • Clean the inlet water filter screen a few times a year
