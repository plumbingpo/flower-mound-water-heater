Keeping Your Fort Worth Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as rusty hot water, popping and rumbling, moisture around the base, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one with water on the floor.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a flooded utility room.
