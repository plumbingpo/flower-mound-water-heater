Most water heaters do not simply wear out; they rust out or scale up, and both problems can be held off for years. As water heats, dissolved minerals come out of the water and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer sits between the flame and the water, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it buries the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, is slowly used up. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Flower Mound, TX, we see this pattern constantly, and most of it was preventable.

We work through a full checklist every time. The tank is drained and flushed to clear the sediment layer. The anode rod comes out for inspection and is replaced when little of it remains. The T&P relief valve is tested so we know it will open under pressure and close again cleanly. On gas models, we check the burner, pilot assembly, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue, and draft. Electric heaters get element and thermostat testing and a check of the wiring connections. We also measure the air charge in the expansion tank, check the water pressure, set the thermostat to a scald-safe level unless a mixing valve allows higher storage, and inspect the drain valve, connections, and pan.

Tankless owners need maintenance too, just a different kind. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers flow problems, fluctuating temperatures, and error codes. We flush the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. When to Call for Water Heater Maintenance

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • Shorter showers before the water turns lukewarm
  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings
  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • Higher gas or electric costs for the same routine
  • Hot water that looks cloudy or rust-colored
  • A water heater that has never been flushed or inspected

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Here's where to begin:

  • Ask about the anode rod at each visit; it is the tank's main defense against rust
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
  • Make an annual flush part of your home maintenance routine
  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
