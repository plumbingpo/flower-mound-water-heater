Keeping Your Denton Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that range and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a quick, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.
