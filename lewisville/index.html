When Your Water Heater Fails in Lewisville

When your hot water disappears, how fast help shows up matters more than anything else. That's why we keep our schedule flexible across the area and put a real person on the phone. Emergency calls typically see a technician within a day, and we treat leaks and no-hot-water calls as top priority.

The emergency calls we answer usually follow a pattern. On gas heaters, it's a pilot that won't stay lit, a burner that won't ignite, or a gas control valve that has failed. On electric heaters, it's a dead element, a faulty high-limit switch, or a breaker that won't stay on. And on any type, it's leaks at the base, the drain valve, or the fittings, plus relief valves that discharge. Because our trucks carry thermocouples, elements, thermostats, gas valves, relief valves, and fittings, the majority of these calls are wrapped up in one visit, with the price agreed before any work begins.

Two common situations call for quick action from you before a technician arrives. The first is a leak: close the cold water valve above the tank and shut off the breaker or gas control, so the tank stops refilling and stops heating. The second is a relief valve that keeps releasing water or a tank that makes loud banging noises: both can mean pressure or temperature running too high, so turn the heater off and keep away from the discharge pipe. If you smell gas at any point, get outside and call your gas utility first. After that, call us at (972) 975-6347. Those few minutes can save you from a much larger repair.

Keeping Your Lewisville Water Heater Running Longer

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and causes far less stress than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
