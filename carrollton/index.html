Keeping Your Carrollton Water Heater Running Longer

Most premature tank failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a quick, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a flooded utility room.
